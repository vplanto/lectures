# Research and Methodological Activities: Research Directions

> 🇺🇦 [Українська версія](../02_research.md) | 🇬🇧 English version

Central hub for research tasks, ideas, and experiments.

1.  **Routing, logistics, and agent-based modeling problems**
    * **Essence:** Research and development of algorithms for solving classical transportation and routing problems (e.g., A\* pathfinding). This direction also includes **agent-based modeling** for simulating complex, dynamic systems (e.g., transportation flows, evacuation processes) to analyze their behavior and identify bottlenecks.

2.  **Time series analysis and predictive monitoring**
    * **Essence:** Application of statistical methods, machine learning, and neural networks (particularly **RNN**) for analyzing time series generated by IT systems (CPU, RAM metrics, etc.). The goal is to build **predictive models** capable of forecasting failures and detecting complex, non-obvious anomalies that classical methods miss.

3.  **Interpretable AI (XAI) and prescriptive analysis**
    * **Essence:** A direction that is the next step after predictive analysis. invece of simply stating "failure" (predictive), the focus shifts to **prescriptive analysis** — generating specific recommendations for system optimization. A key part is research into **interpretability** (of neural network "black boxes") using methods like SHAP or LIME to explain anomaly causes and justify proposed solutions.

4.  **Monitoring and analysis of high-load distributed systems**
    * **Essence:** Research and development of comprehensive tools for monitoring modern distributed systems (e.g., Kubernetes). This includes both classical approaches (statistical metric analysis) and modern ones, particularly applying **large language models (LLM)** for automatic log and event interpretation, problem diagnosis, and providing recommendations to engineers.

5.  **Semantic analysis and natural language processing (NLP) in technical domains**
    * **Essence:** Application of natural language processing models (from **Naive Bayes** to transformers like **BERT** and **LLM**) for working with unstructured technical data. The direction includes tasks of semantic filtering of "information noise" from logs, automatic event classification, and understanding engineers' queries in natural language.

6. **Game theory and adversarial modeling for distributed system resilience**
    * **Essence:** Application of game theory mathematical apparatus (e.g., zero-sum games, Minimax) for analyzing and improving resilience (robustness) of distributed systems in competitive or unpredictable conditions.
    * **Directions:**
    * **Robust resource planning:** Development of scheduler algorithms, considered as a "player" trying to optimally distribute tasks. An "adversarial" simulator opposes it, generating worst-case but realistic load scenarios or node failures. The goal is to find a planning strategy that is effective even under worst-case conditions.
    * **Security and intrusion detection:** Modeling interaction between intrusion detection systems (IDS) and simulated "adversary". Using adversarial algorithms to develop security policies that are resilient to new or atypical attack vectors.

## Update: January 2026. AI-Mathematics and Lean in Technical Domains

## 1. The Terence Tao Phenomenon: From Skepticism to "Copilot for Proofs"

For a mathematician of Terence Tao's caliber, the transition to using AI wasn't a matter of automating calculations, but a matter of **formalizing intuition**. Until 2024, AI was perceived in academic circles as a "probabilistic parrot" prone to hallucinations in logical transitions. However, the release of models with "reasoning" architecture (such as o1 and GPT-5.2) radically changed the situation.

**What surprised Tao:**
* **Zero-shot Originality:** AI began proposing not just known proofs, but original logical steps in complex problems (IMO level and higher) that are not part of the training dataset.
* **Semantic Parsing of Logic:** The ability of models to translate "raw" mathematical ideas from natural language into formal Lean code without losing semantic precision.
* **Exploration of Limits:** Tao noted that AI is now capable of identifying "bottlenecks" in proofs, proposing counterexamples that previously required months of human analysis.

This creates a new paradigm: AI doesn't replace the mathematician but becomes a **low-latency interface** between idea and its formal verification. For us, this means the ability to quickly verify algorithm robustness before they reach Production.

## 2. Lean: Architecture of Truth (Formal Verification 101)

Lean is not just a language; it's an **interactive theorem prover** built on calculus of inductive constructions. In a world where high-load systems suffer from non-determinism, Lean serves as the "ultimate arbiter" of correctness.

**Key technical characteristics:**
* **Correctness by Construction:** Code in Lean cannot be compiled if it is logically inconsistent. This eliminates classes of runtime errors that cannot be caught by tests.
* **Dependent Types:** Data types in Lean can depend on values. For example, the type "array of length $n$" allows proving at compile time that the index will never go out of bounds (Buffer Overflow Prevention).
* **The Kernel:** At the core of Lean lies a very small, formally verified kernel. Even if an AI agent proposes a hallucination, Lean's kernel will reject it, as every step must conform to the axioms of logic.

**Mathematical foundation (Type Theory):**
In Lean, every statement $P$ is viewed as a type, and proof $p$ as an element of that type:
$$p : P$$
If you can construct element $p$, you've proven theorem $P$. This transforms mathematics into software development, where "compilation" means "truth."

## 3. Synergy: Lean + VRP and Agent-Based Modeling

In logistics problems such as **VRP (Vehicle Routing Problem)** and **VRPTW (Time Windows)**, the industry typically relies on heuristics (e.g., Clarke-Wright algorithm or meta-heuristics like Tabu Search). However, in high-load systems and complex supply chains, these heuristics often contain logical "corners" that lead to unpredictable behavior during cascading load growth.

**Verification of pathfinding algorithms (A*):**
Using Lean allows transforming a search algorithm from "probabilistically optimal" to **formally proven**. We can mathematically verify that the chosen heuristic $h(n)$ is **admissible** and **consistent** for a specific state graph:
$$h(n) \le c(n, a) + h(a)$$
Where:
* $c(n, a)$ — real cost of transition from node $n$ to adjacent node $a$.
* $h(n)$ — estimated cost to goal.

Proving this inequality in Lean guarantees that your scheduler will always find the global cost minimum, which is critical for FinOps optimization in distributed systems.

**Verification of agent strategies:**
In **agent-based modeling** direction, the problem of "live-locks" and deadlocks arises when hundreds of independent nodes interact.
* **Safety Properties:** Through Lean, we can formally prove safety invariants — states the system should **never** enter (e.g., collision of two agents' routes).
* **Liveness Verification:** Proving that every order or data packet will necessarily reach its destination in finite steps $N < \infty$.

**Practical application:**
AI models (o1/GPT-5.2) are now capable of not just writing Python code for solving VRP, but simultaneously generating **Proof-script** in Lean. This allows implementing architecture where routing code is updated dynamically but passes through a Lean verifier "sieve" before Production deployment.

## 4. Formalizing Chaos: Verification of Predictive Models

Predictive monitoring of complex IT systems is based on the hypothesis that metric time series (CPU, Latency, RAM) are not purely stochastic but contain a deterministic component or "memory." Using the Lean tool allows transitioning from empirical observation of anomalies to formal proof of predictive model properties.

**Mathematical verification of Hurst exponent ($H$):**
For system state diagnosis, R/S analysis is applied, allowing determination of time series persistence degree. In the Lean environment, formal verification of the $H$ calculation algorithm is implemented, guaranteeing absence of errors caused by seasonality or sampling artifacts:
* **$H = 0.5$:** Formal proof that the series is random walk (Brownian motion), for which predictive modeling is impossible.
* **$0.5 < H \le 1$:** Verification of long-term memory (persistence) presence, justifying application of LSTM-type architectures.

**Verification of neural network robustness (LSTM/RNN):**
One of the main problems of deep learning in critical systems is "black box" status. However, LSTM architecture can be viewed as a system of nonlinear differential equations. Using Lean makes it possible to:
1.  **Prove Lyapunov stability:** Verification that hidden states of the network remain within bounded limits and don't enter chaotic oscillation states during cascading input data growth.
2.  **Bound reconstruction error:** Formalization of dynamic anomaly threshold. Proving a theorem that under given training parameters $\theta$, error $|y_{real} - y_{pred}|$ will not exceed $3\sigma$ in absence of structural system failures.

**Formalizing "memory" as invariant:**
Within predictive monitoring, system "memory" is defined as a mathematical invariant. Lean allows describing Forget Gate and Input Gate mechanisms in LSTM as logical predicates regulating information flow. This ensures deterministic model behavior when processing long sequences (logs over large time periods), eliminating vanishing gradient problem at logical architecture design stage.

Thus, integrating Lean into predictive model development process transforms SRE practice from reactive response to formally verified catastrophic state prevention system (Chaos Prevention).

## 5. LLM as a Bridge Between Logs and Formal Specifications (K8s Case)

Modern high-load distributed systems, particularly Kubernetes, generate arrays of unstructured data (logs, events, metrics), the volume of which complicates classical statistical analysis. Applying large language models (LLM) in this context allows automating system state interpretation and translating malfunction symptoms into formal specifications for further verification.

**Semantic filtering and analysis process:**
At the data preparation stage (Dataset Engineering), event classification is performed using BERT-type models or specialized LLMs. This provides:
* **Information noise removal:** Filtering duplicate or non-informative log records, allowing focus of computational resources on critical anomalies.
* **Hidden dependency detection:** LLMs can recognize cascading failures (e.g., OOMKilled analysis) where one node's failure cause is hidden in another service's logs.

**Transformation into formal proofs:**
Key innovation is using LLM as translator between natural language (log linguistics) and formal verification code (Lean). The obtained symptom sequence is transformed into a mathematical model of current cluster state, allowing:
1.  **Diagnosis through verification:** AI proposes hypothesis about anomaly cause, and Lean tool verifies it for logical consistency relative to system architectural invariants.
2.  **Automatic recommendation generation:** Instead of probabilistic assumptions, the system provides prescriptive solutions (e.g., resource limit changes) that mathematically guarantee stability of subsequent operation.

**FinOps and resource optimization:**
Integrating LLM into monitoring processes promotes deeper cost analysis in cloud environments. AI agents analyze resource consumption patterns and, based on formally verified forecasting models, propose scaling strategies that minimize financial costs without risk of violating SLA (Service Level Agreement).

Thus, using LLM combined with Lean formal logic creates a deterministic bridge between chaotic logs of distributed systems and strict engineering analysis specifications.

## 6. Game Theory and Adversarial Modeling for Distributed System Resilience

Applying game theory mathematical apparatus, particularly zero-sum games and Minimax algorithm, allows conducting deep analysis and improving resilience (robustness) of distributed systems in competitive or unpredictable conditions. Formalizing such interactions in Lean environment transforms strategic planning into deterministic optimization task with mathematically guaranteed result.

### Robust Resource Planning
Within adversarial modeling, scheduler is viewed as a "player" whose strategic goal is optimal task distribution. An adversarial simulator opposes it, generating worst-case but realistic load scenarios or node failures.

Mathematical formalization of the task is implemented through cost function:
$$\min_{s \in S} \max_{a \in A} \text{Cost}(s, a)$$
Where:
* $s \in S$ — planning strategy space.
* $a \in A$ — adversarial load scenario space.

Modern AI models demonstrate capability to solve high-level mathematical problems of this type, proposing original strategies and proofs that go beyond classical heuristics. This allows finding global minima in complex optimization tasks faster than traditional methods.

### Strategy Verification Through Lean
Using interactive theorem provers such as Lean allows formally proving that the chosen planning strategy is Minimax-optimal or corresponds to Nash equilibrium state. This eliminates logical brittleness problem of algorithms inherent to probabilistic methods and guarantees system operation correctness under any admissible adversary actions.

### Security and Intrusion Detection (IDS)
Adversarial modeling is fundamental for analyzing interaction between intrusion detection systems (IDS) and simulated adversary. Using adversarial algorithms allows developing security policies that are resilient to new or atypical attack vectors.

Formal verification of such games guarantees:
* **Policy robustness:** Proving that the system remains in safe state (safety property) under any adversary strategy.
* **Response optimality:** Mathematical justification of countermeasure selection based on formally verified logic, not probabilistic assumptions.

Integrating AI capable of complex logical synthesis together with formal verification transforms resilient system design into a process of building mathematically impeccable architectures.
